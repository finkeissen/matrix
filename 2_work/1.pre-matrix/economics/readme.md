# economics

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to economics**.

It is a structured stress environment for:
- economic models and theories
- empirical measurements and indicators
- assumptions about agents, markets, and institutions
- normative and descriptive entanglements
- policy-relevant claims (as claims, not prescriptions)

This domain does **not** represent economic truth or advice.
It represents **what is claimed, by whom, when,
under which assumptions, and in conflict with what**.

---

## Why Economics Is Treated as a Primary Domain

Economics is an **epistemic regime** with distinctive tensions:

- strong dependence on modeling assumptions
- entanglement of description and normativity
- use of idealized agents and abstractions
- partial and theory-laden data
- real-world impact of theoretical claims

These tensions make economics
structurally fragile and epistemically risky,
and therefore a critical stress domain.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- micro- and macroeconomic models
- theoretical frameworks and schools
- empirical studies and indicators
- assumptions about rationality and behavior
- historical and contemporary economic debates
- critiques and counter-models

Inclusion does not imply endorsement or applicability.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- a policy recommendation system
- a guide for economic decision-making
- a consensus representation
- a ranking of theories or schools
- a substitute for political judgment

No model or framework is privileged by default.

---

## Conflict and Normativity

The economics domain explicitly permits:

- incompatible models
- conflicting empirical claims
- disputes over assumptions
- tension between descriptive and normative claims
- parallel schools of thought

Normativity must be explicit.
Implicit normativity is treated as structural failure.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

Economics may overlap with:
- politics and governance
- psychology and behavior
- sociology and society
- technology and infrastructure
- mathematics and modeling

Cross-domain conflict is expected and permitted.

---

## Relationship to Runs

No content appears in this domain
without at least one corresponding run.

Runs document:
- inputs
- constraints
- produced artifacts
- surfaced conflicts and gaps

Unrun content belongs in `work/`.

---

## Known Open Questions (Indicative)

This list is intentionally incomplete.

Examples include:
- limits of modeling under real-world complexity
- role of assumptions in empirical claims
- interaction between theory and policy
- persistence of conflicting schools

These questions are represented, not answered.

---

## Summary

> **This domain does not explain the economy.
> It explains how the economy is explained in economics.**

It exists to expose structure, assumptions, and conflict —
not to legitimize decisions.
