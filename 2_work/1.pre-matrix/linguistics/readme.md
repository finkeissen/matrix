# linguistics

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to linguistics**.

It is a structured stress environment for:
- claims about language, meaning, and communication
- formal and informal models of language
- descriptive, theoretical, and applied frameworks
- methodological and empirical disputes
- interfaces between language, cognition, and society

This domain does **not** represent linguistic truth.
It represents **what is claimed, by whom, when,
under which assumptions, and in conflict with what**.

---

## Why Linguistics Is Treated as a Primary Domain

Linguistics is an **epistemic regime** with distinctive properties:

- strong dependence on definitions and formalisms
- tension between description and prescription
- plurality of incompatible theories
- deep interaction with cognition, culture, and technology
- language-dependent formulation of its own objects

These properties make linguistics structurally reflexive
and epistemically demanding.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- phonetics and phonology
- morphology and syntax
- semantics and pragmatics
- discourse and text analysis
- sociolinguistics and variation
- computational and formal linguistics
- historical and comparative linguistics

Inclusion does not imply endorsement or universality.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- a normative grammar
- a language standard
- a prescriptive usage guide
- a canonical theory of meaning
- a ranking of linguistic frameworks

No language, model, or theory is privileged by default.

---

## Conflict and Plurality

The linguistics domain explicitly permits:

- competing theories of meaning
- incompatible formalisms
- divergent empirical generalizations
- disputes over data and methodology
- parallel descriptive traditions

Conflict is expected.
Resolution is not required.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

Linguistics may overlap with:
- psychology and cognition
- informatics and AI
- culture and society
- history and anthropology
- philosophy and logic

Boundaries are pragmatic and reversible.

## Summary

> **This domain does not define language.
> It describes how language is described in linguistics.**

It exists to expose structure, assumptions, and disagreement —
not to enforce interpretation.
