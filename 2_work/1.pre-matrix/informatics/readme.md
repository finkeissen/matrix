# informatics

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to informatics**.

Informatics here includes:
- computation and formal systems
- information structures and representations
- software and systems engineering (as claims, not prescriptions)
- data, models, and implementation realities

This domain does **not** represent correctness of systems or code.
It represents **what is claimed, by whom, when,
under which assumptions, and in conflict with what**.

---

## Why Informatics Is Treated as a Primary Domain

Informatics is an **epistemic regime** with distinctive stress conditions:

- formal correctness vs. practical behavior
- specification vs. implementation divergence
- context-dependent performance and failure
- hidden assumptions encoded in tooling and standards
- rapid evolution and temporal instability
- strong coupling between language and structure

These conditions make informatics structurally demanding
and central to the Matrix’s self-reference.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- formal models and semantics
- algorithmic claims and complexity statements
- system architectures and design patterns (as descriptions)
- software behavior, constraints, and failure modes
- data representation formats and transformations
- AI systems as computational artifacts (not as authority)

Inclusion does not imply endorsement.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- a best-practice guide
- a normative standard library
- a security policy
- a software engineering handbook
- an optimization or decision framework

No method or tool is privileged by default.

---

## Conflict and Divergence

The informatics domain explicitly permits:

- competing formalisms
- incompatible definitions of correctness
- conflicting empirical performance claims
- disputes between specification and implementation
- multiple valid representations of the same process

Conflict is expected.
Resolution is not required.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

Informatics may overlap with:
- mathematics and logic
- physics (hardware and measurement)
- technology and engineering
- economics and politics (infrastructure and governance)
- meta-domains (self-description)

Boundaries are pragmatic and reversible.

## Relationship to Runs

No content appears in this domain
without at least one corresponding run.

Runs document:
- inputs
- constraints
- produced artifacts
- surfaced conflicts and gaps

Unrun content belongs in `work/`.

## Known Open Questions (Indicative)

This list is intentionally incomplete.

Examples include:
- when specifications become implicitly normative
- how to represent implementation divergence structurally
- how to preserve provenance through transformations
- how to represent temporal instability of standards and tools

These questions are represented, not answered.

---

## Summary

> **This domain does not certify systems.
> It describes how systems are described in informatics.**

It exists to expose structure under constraint,
not to collapse it into authority.
