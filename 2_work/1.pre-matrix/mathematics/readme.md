# mathematics

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to mathematics**.

It is a structured stress environment for:
- formal systems
- axioms and definitions
- proofs and proof strategies
- models and abstractions
- foundational disputes and interpretations

This domain does **not** represent mathematical truth.
It represents **what is asserted, formalized, proved, disputed, or assumed,
by whom, when, and under which axiomatic commitments**.

---

## Why Mathematics Is Treated as a Primary Domain

Mathematics is a paradigmatic **epistemic regime**:

- it operates without empirical objects
- it relies on explicit axioms and inference rules
- it allows multiple incompatible foundations
- it separates formal validity from interpretation
- it produces certainty locally, but not globally

These properties make mathematics
structurally central and epistemically non-trivial.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- axiomatic systems
- formal definitions
- proofs and proof outlines
- alternative foundations
- meta-mathematical claims
- disputes about consistency, completeness, or interpretation

Inclusion does not imply acceptance or canonical status.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- a canonical foundation of knowledge
- a hierarchy of “correct” mathematics
- a unified formal system
- a replacement for proof verification
- a claim that mathematics is epistemically privileged

No axiom system is privileged by default.

---

## Plurality and Incompatibility

The mathematics domain explicitly permits:

- incompatible axiom systems
- competing foundational programs
- different notions of proof
- alternative formalizations of similar ideas

Coexistence is expected.
Unification is not required.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

Mathematics may overlap with:
- physics and informatics
- economics and social modeling
- logic and philosophy
- technology and engineering

These overlaps are representable,
but never silently collapsed.

## Summary

> **This domain does not ground certainty.
> It describes how certainty is constructed in mathematics.**

It exists to expose formal structure and its limits,
not to serve as an ultimate foundation.
