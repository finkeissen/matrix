# history

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to history**.

It is a structured stress environment for:
- claims about past events, processes, and structures
- sources and their interpretation
- narrative frameworks and periodizations
- methodological disputes and historiography
- temporal scope and uncertainty

This domain does **not** represent historical truth.
It represents **what is claimed about the past,
by whom, when,
under which sources and assumptions,
and in conflict with what**.

---

## Why History Is Treated as a Primary Domain

History is an **epistemic regime** defined by:

- irreversibility of its object
- dependence on fragmentary sources
- interpretive mediation of evidence
- narrative construction under constraint
- strong coupling between time, meaning, and perspective

These features make history structurally distinct
from both empirical sciences and formal systems.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- historical claims and interpretations
- primary and secondary sources (as provenance)
- periodizations and temporal frameworks
- historiographical debates
- methodological reflections
- counterfactual and comparative history (as claims)

Inclusion does not imply acceptance or completeness.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- an objective record of the past
- a definitive timeline
- a consensus narrative
- a ranking of sources
- a substitute for archival practice

No narrative or interpretation is privileged by default.

---

## Conflict and Multiplicity

The history domain explicitly permits:

- conflicting interpretations
- incompatible narratives
- disputed sources
- competing periodizations
- parallel historical accounts

Conflict is expected.
Resolution is not required.

---

## Temporal Structure

Temporal scope is central to this domain.

Claims must be explicit about:
- the time they address
- the periodization assumed
- the limits of available sources

Temporal gaps are expected and preserved.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

History may overlap with:
- politics and society
- economics and technology
- culture and religion
- linguistics and anthropology
- meta-domains and self-reference

Cross-domain conflict is expected and permitted.

---

## Relationship to Runs

No content appears in this domain
without at least one corresponding run.

Runs document:
- inputs
- constraints
- produced artifacts
- surfaced conflicts and gaps

Unrun content belongs in `work/`.

---

## Known Open Questions (Indicative)

This list is intentionally incomplete.

Examples include:
- limits of source-based inference
- role of narrative structure
- treatment of silence and absence
- relationship between history and memory

These questions are represented, not answered.

---

## Summary

> **This domain does not reconstruct the past.
> It describes how the past is reconstructed in history.**

It exists to preserve temporal structure,
interpretive plurality,
and epistemic limits.
