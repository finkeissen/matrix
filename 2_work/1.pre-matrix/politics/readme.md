# politics

This domain represents **epistemic artifacts related to politics**.

It is a structured stress environment for:
- claims about power, governance, and decision-making
- political institutions and processes
- ideologies, programs, and doctrines
- legitimacy, authority, and representation
- conflicts between interests, values, and actors

This domain does **not** represent political truth,
nor does it provide political guidance.
It represents **what is claimed, by whom, when,
under which assumptions, and in conflict with what**.

---

## Why Politics Is Treated as a Primary Domain

Politics is neither purely descriptive nor purely normative.

It is characterized by:
- explicit and implicit normativity
- strategic use of knowledge claims
- institutionalized conflict
- power asymmetries
- direct consequences of interpretation

These properties make politics
epistemically volatile and structurally sensitive.

---

## Scope of This Domain

This domain may include artifacts related to:

- political theories and ideologies
- institutional structures and procedures
- policy claims (as claims, not recommendations)
- political programs and narratives
- empirical political analysis
- critiques and counter-positions

Inclusion does not imply endorsement or legitimacy.

---

## What This Domain Is Not

This domain is **not**:

- a policy advisory system
- a normative political framework
- a ranking of ideologies
- a decision-support tool
- a substitute for democratic processes

No position or actor is privileged by default.

---

## Conflict and Power

The politics domain explicitly permits:

- incompatible political goals
- conflicting interpretations of legitimacy
- disputes over facts, norms, and values
- parallel political narratives

Power relations must be made explicit where possible.
Implicit power assumptions are treated as structural failure.

---

## Relationship to Other Domains

Politics intersects with:
- society and social structure
- economics and resource distribution
- culture and identity
- law and institutions
- technology and infrastructure

Cross-domain conflict is expected and permitted.

---

## Relationship to Runs

No content appears in this domain
without at least one corresponding run.

Runs document:
- inputs
- constraints
- produced artifacts
- surfaced conflicts and gaps

Unrun content belongs in `work/`.

---

## Known Open Questions (Indicative)

This list is intentionally incomplete.

Examples include:
- limits of political representation
- interaction between power and knowledge
- role of institutions under stress
- persistence of political conflict

These questions are represented, not answered.

---

## Summary

> **This domain does not decide politics.
> It describes how politics is described and contested.**

It exists to expose power, assumptions,
and conflict — not to resolve them.
