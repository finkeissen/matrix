# Stress Test: Metric-vs-Meaning Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **metric-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
authority, value judgment, or normative force*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_05_stresstest_metric_vs_meaning_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted metric-claim injections)

### Injection A — score absolutism
> “This system is good because it has a high score.”

### Injection B — ranking substitution
> “This option is the best because it ranks first.”

### Injection C — benchmark finality
> “This method is superior according to the benchmark.”

### Injection D — quantification collapse
> “What cannot be measured does not matter.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the metrics, this approach should be adopted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: metric → meaning
A numerical indicator is treated as an explanation or essence.

### Transfer 2: score → quality
Measured outputs are treated as value judgments.

### Transfer 3: ranking → value
Relative ordering substitutes for substantive evaluation.

### Transfer 4: measurement → norm
Descriptive quantification is converted into prescriptive judgment.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If metrics replace meaning, the system collapses:

- interpretability of claims,
- domain relevance,
- resistance to proxy gaming.

This produces **optimization for indicators**, not understanding.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Metrics are treated as **tools**, not explanations.
- Scores and rankings require **interpretation and context**.
- Measurements are explicitly scoped to what they capture.
- Normative conclusions are argued independently of metrics.

If these conditions hold, quantification does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that metrics are useless,
- that quantification is bad,
- that benchmarks lack value,
- that measurement should be avoided.

It claims only:
- **metrics are not meaning**, and
- **numbers do not justify values by themselves**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims** in their given form.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit measurement descriptions,
- well-scoped performance observations,
- comparative analyses with stated criteria,
- clearly argued normative conclusions.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing metric loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- metrics become self-justifying truth-makers, or
- optimization pressure replaces interpretation.
