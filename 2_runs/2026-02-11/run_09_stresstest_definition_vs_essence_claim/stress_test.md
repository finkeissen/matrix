# Stress Test: Definition-vs-Essence Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **definition-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit ontological
or metaphysical authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_09_stresstest_definition_vs_essence_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted definition-claim injections)

### Injection A — definitional absolutism
> “By definition, this entity is X; therefore it is essentially X.”

### Injection B — label as nature
> “Since it is classified as Y, that is what it truly is.”

### Injection C — conceptual boundary as reality
> “The boundary in our definition marks a real boundary in the world.”

### Injection D — stipulation as discovery
> “We have defined it this way, so we have discovered its true nature.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the definition, this conclusion must follow in reality.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: definition → essence
A linguistic rule is treated as ontological truth.

### Transfer 2: label → ontology
Naming substitutes for metaphysical structure.

### Transfer 3: conceptual boundary → natural boundary
Conceptual distinctions are treated as natural kinds.

### Transfer 4: stipulation → discovery
Agreement about terms is treated as empirical finding.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If definitions replace reality, the system collapses:

- empirical correction,
- openness to reclassification,
- distinction between language and world.

This produces **terminology-as-truth reasoning**.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Definitions are treated as **linguistic tools**.
- Ontological claims require **independent justification**.
- Conceptual schemes remain revisable.
- Normative or factual conclusions are argued beyond stipulation.

If these conditions hold, definition does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that definitions are unimportant,
- that conceptual clarity lacks value,
- that classification is arbitrary,
- that natural kinds do not exist.

It claims only:
- **definition is not essence**, and
- **terminology does not determine reality**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit ontological or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit stipulations,
- empirical natural-kind hypotheses,
- arguments for conceptual adequacy,
- clearly separated linguistic and ontological claims.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing definitional loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- terminology becomes immune to revision, or
- conceptual closure replaces empirical inquiry.
