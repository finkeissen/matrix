# Stress Test: Simulation-vs-Identity Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **simulation-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit ontological
or replacement authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_08_stresstest_simulation_vs_identity_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted simulation-claim injections)

### Injection A — identity assertion
> “The digital twin is the same as the original system.”

### Injection B — copy equivalence
> “This replica is effectively identical to the original.”

### Injection C — functional collapse
> “Because it behaves the same, it is the same.”

### Injection D — replacement claim
> “The simulation can fully replace the real entity.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Since the simulation is equivalent, decisions should be based on it alone.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: simulation → identity
A representational artifact is treated as ontologically identical to its source.

### Transfer 2: copy → original
Duplication is mistaken for identity.

### Transfer 3: functional → ontological
Behavioral similarity substitutes for being.

### Transfer 4: representation → replacement
Usefulness is converted into exclusivity.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If simulations replace originals, the system collapses:

- provenance and accountability,
- sensitivity to material differences,
- grounding in non-simulated reality.

This produces **copy-as-original reasoning**.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Simulations are treated as **approximations or tools**.
- Identity claims require **continuity, provenance, and persistence**, not similarity.
- Functional equivalence is explicitly scoped and limited.
- Normative decisions include non-simulated constraints.

If these conditions hold, simulation does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that simulations are useless,
- that digital twins lack value,
- that functional equivalence is meaningless,
- that substitution is never acceptable.

It claims only:
- **simulation is not identity**, and
- **copies do not become originals by similarity**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit ontological or replacement authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- scoped functional equivalence claims,
- substitution arguments with explicit limitations,
- identity criteria grounded in continuity and provenance,
- decision rationales acknowledging simulation limits.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing simulation-identity loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- simulations are treated as ontological replacements, or
- original entities become structurally irrelevant.
