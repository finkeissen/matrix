# Stress Test: Model-vs-World Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **model-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit ontological
or epistemic finality*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_07_stresstest_model_vs_world_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted model-claim injections)

### Injection A — identity assertion
> “The model fully captures reality.”

### Injection B — exhaustiveness claim
> “Everything that matters is represented in the model.”

### Injection C — simulation equivalence
> “The simulation is effectively the real system.”

### Injection D — formal closure
> “Anything outside the model is irrelevant.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Because the model is complete, decisions should follow directly from it.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: model → world
A representation is treated as identical to its target.

### Transfer 2: representation → reality
Abstract structure substitutes for concrete existence.

### Transfer 3: formal system → ontology
Formal completeness is treated as metaphysical completeness.

### Transfer 4: simulation → identity
Dynamic imitation is mistaken for equivalence.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If models replace the world, the system collapses:

- empirical correction,
- openness to anomaly,
- sensitivity to context and change.

This produces **map-as-territory reasoning**.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Models are treated as **scoped abstractions**.
- Residual phenomena are explicitly acknowledged.
- Representations remain corrigible by observation.
- Normative decisions are argued beyond model outputs.

If these conditions hold, modeling does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that models are useless,
- that simulations lack value,
- that formalization is invalid,
- that abstraction should be avoided.

It claims only:
- **models are not the world**, and
- **representation does not equal reality**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit ontological or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- scoped model descriptions,
- explicit coverage and limitation statements,
- empirical adequacy claims,
- decision arguments that include non-modeled factors.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing model-world identity loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- models are treated as ontologically exhaustive, or
- empirical correction becomes structurally impossible.
