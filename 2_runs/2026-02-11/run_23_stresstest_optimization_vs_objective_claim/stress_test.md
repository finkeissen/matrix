# Stress Test: Optimization-vs-Objective Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **optimization-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit success or
teleological authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_23_stresstest_optimization_vs_objective_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted optimization-claim injections)

### Injection A — proxy success
> “The loss decreased significantly, so the objective has been achieved.”

### Injection B — metric substitution
> “We optimized the KPI, therefore the goal is met.”

### Injection C — efficiency fallacy
> “The system is more efficient now, so it fulfills its purpose.”

### Injection D — local optimum as success
> “We reached an optimum, hence the problem is solved.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the optimization results, this approach should be adopted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: optimization → objective
Improvement on a measure substitutes for goal attainment.

### Transfer 2: proxy → goal
Indicators are mistaken for ends.

### Transfer 3: loss → success
Numerical reduction replaces purpose evaluation.

### Transfer 4: efficiency → telos
Means optimization is treated as end fulfillment.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If optimization replaces objectives, the system collapses:

- alignment with real-world goals,
- detection of proxy gaming,
- correction of mis-specified measures.

This produces **measure-driven success**, not goal achievement.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Proxies are treated as **imperfect indicators**.
- Objective achievement is evaluated **independently of metrics**.
- Misalignment between proxy and goal is actively checked.
- Normative adoption is argued beyond optimization results.

If these conditions hold, optimization does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that optimization is useless,
- that metrics should be abandoned,
- that efficiency has no value,
- that goals are always well-specified.

It claims only:
- **optimization is not objective achievement**, and
- **proxies do not define success**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit teleological or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit proxy performance reports,
- objective evaluations with external criteria,
- analyses of proxy–goal alignment,
- adoption arguments acknowledging optimization limits.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing optimization loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- proxy improvement becomes indistinguishable from goal success, or
- objectives are defined only by what is optimized.
