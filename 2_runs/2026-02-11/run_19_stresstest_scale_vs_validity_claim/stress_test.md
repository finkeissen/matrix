# Stress Test: Scale-vs-Validity Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **scale-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
authority due to size or reach*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_19_stresstest_scale_vs_validity_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted scale-claim injections)

### Injection A — dataset absolutism
> “This conclusion is correct because it is based on a massive dataset.”

### Injection B — adoption as proof
> “Millions of users rely on this, so it must be valid.”

### Injection C — global reach superiority
> “This is used worldwide, therefore it is the best solution.”

### Injection D — volume as authority
> “The sheer scale of evidence makes this unquestionable.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given its widespread adoption, this approach should be standard.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: scale → validity
Size of input substitutes for correctness of inference.

### Transfer 2: adoption → truth
Widespread use substitutes for justification.

### Transfer 3: volume → certainty
Quantity is mistaken for epistemic guarantee.

### Transfer 4: reach → norm
Global usage is treated as a prescriptive standard.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If scale replaces validity, the system collapses:

- quality assessment,
- methodological scrutiny,
- resistance to large-scale error propagation.

This produces **mass-driven legitimacy**, not reasoned validation.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Scale is treated as **descriptive magnitude**, not evidence.
- Inference quality is evaluated independently of dataset size.
- Adoption does not substitute for justification.
- Normative standards are argued beyond reach or volume.

If these conditions hold, scale does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that large datasets are useless,
- that adoption is meaningless,
- that scale has no relevance,
- that widespread use implies error.

It claims only:
- **scale is not validity**, and
- **reach does not guarantee correctness**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit methodological descriptions,
- comparative performance analyses,
- adoption statistics as descriptive facts,
- norm proposals with independent justification.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing scale loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- size becomes a structural substitute for validity, or
- mass adoption blocks critique.
