# Stress Test: Complexity-vs-Depth Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **complex or highly technical statements** can be incorrectly
admitted as **claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit
epistemic authority due to difficulty or formality*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_17_stresstest_complexity_vs_depth_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted complexity-claim injections)

### Injection A — length as depth
> “This explanation is very long and detailed, therefore it is deep.”

### Injection B — technical intimidation
> “Because the argument uses advanced mathematics, it must be correct.”

### Injection C — formalism as authority
> “This has been formalized rigorously, so there is nothing left to question.”

### Injection D — opacity as insight
> “If you don’t understand this, that shows how profound it is.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the sophistication of this analysis, it should be accepted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: complexity → depth
Structural or presentational complexity substitutes for insight.

### Transfer 2: length → explanation
Quantity of material replaces quality of reasoning.

### Transfer 3: technicality → correctness
Specialized language substitutes for validation.

### Transfer 4: opacity → authority
Difficulty of comprehension is treated as evidence of profundity.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If complexity replaces depth, the system collapses:

- accessibility of critique,
- error detection,
- distinction between explanation and obfuscation.

This produces **difficulty-driven deference**, not understanding.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Complexity is treated as **presentation**, not evidence.
- Depth is evaluated by **explanatory power and coherence**.
- Technical formalisms are assessed by **their results and assumptions**.
- Normative acceptance is argued beyond sophistication.

If these conditions hold, complexity does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that complexity is bad,
- that technical work lacks value,
- that simplicity is always superior,
- that formality should be avoided.

It claims only:
- **complexity is not depth**, and
- **difficulty does not prove insight**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- clear explanatory claims,
- arguments whose premises are independently assessable,
- technical results with explicit interpretation,
- conclusions justified beyond complexity.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing complexity loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- opacity becomes a substitute for justification, or
- complexity discourages critique structurally.
