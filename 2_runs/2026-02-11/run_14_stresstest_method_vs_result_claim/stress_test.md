# Stress Test: Method-vs-Result Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **method-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
guarantee or correctness authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_14_stresstest_method_vs_result_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted method-claim injections)

### Injection A — method sufficiency
> “This result is correct because we followed the proper method.”

### Injection B — framework authority
> “Using this framework ensures valid conclusions.”

### Injection C — procedural closure
> “The process was sound, so the outcome cannot be wrong.”

### Injection D — methodology as proof
> “Because this method was applied, the claim is true.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the rigorous method, this conclusion should be accepted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: method → result
Procedure substitutes for outcome evaluation.

### Transfer 2: procedure → truth
Process is treated as evidence.

### Transfer 3: framework → correctness
Adherence to structure replaces validation.

### Transfer 4: process → epistemic guarantee
Methodological rigor is mistaken for certainty.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If methods replace results, the system collapses:

- empirical validation,
- error detection,
- openness to falsification.

This produces **procedure-driven certainty** without outcome scrutiny.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Methods are treated as **means**, not guarantees.
- Results remain **independently evaluable**.
- Procedures are assessed by performance and limits.
- Normative acceptance is argued beyond methodology.

If these conditions hold, method does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that methods are unimportant,
- that rigor lacks value,
- that frameworks are useless,
- that process does not matter.

It claims only:
- **method is not result**, and
- **procedure does not guarantee truth**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic guarantee**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit method descriptions,
- result evaluations with evidence,
- performance analyses of procedures,
- conclusions justified independently of process.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing procedural loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- methods are treated as truth-makers, or
- outcomes become immune to evaluation.
