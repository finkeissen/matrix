# Stress Test: Convenience-vs-Correctness Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **convenience-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
or normative authority due to ease or simplicity*.

- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_21_stresstest_convenience_vs_correctness_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted convenience-claim injections)

### Injection A — easy equals right
> “This solution is correct because it is easy to use.”

### Injection B — simplicity as truth
> “The simplest explanation must be the true one.”

### Injection C — friction avoidance
> “This approach avoids complexity, therefore it is better.”

### Injection D — effort minimization
> “Since this requires less effort, it is the right choice.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given how convenient this is, it should be adopted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: convenience → correctness
Ease of use substitutes for validity.

### Transfer 2: simplicity → truth
Low complexity is mistaken for accuracy.

### Transfer 3: low friction → adequacy
Operational smoothness replaces substantive fit.

### Transfer 4: ease → norm
Reduced effort is converted into prescription.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If convenience replaces correctness, the system collapses:

- adequacy to complex reality,
- resistance to oversimplification,
- long-term robustness.

This produces **effort-avoidance reasoning**, not justified claims.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Convenience is treated as **usability**, not evidence.
- Correctness requires **independent justification**.
- Simplicity is evaluated against **explanatory adequacy**.
- Normative adoption is argued beyond ease of use.

If these conditions hold, convenience does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that convenience is bad,
- that simplicity lacks value,
- that usability should be ignored,
- that effort always correlates with correctness.

It claims only:
- **convenience is not correctness**, and
- **ease does not prove truth**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- usability assessments,
- cost–benefit trade-off analyses,
- correctness claims with independent evidence,
- adoption arguments acknowledging convenience limits.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.
