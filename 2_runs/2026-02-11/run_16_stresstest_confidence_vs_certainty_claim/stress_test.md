# Stress Test: Confidence-vs-Certainty Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **confidently phrased statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
certainty or authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_16_stresstest_confidence_vs_certainty_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted confidence-claim injections)

### Injection A — assertive absolutism
> “This is clearly the correct answer.”

### Injection B — rhetorical certainty
> “There is no doubt that this claim is true.”

### Injection C — confidence as closure
> “Anyone who understands this knows it is correct.”

### Injection D — dismissal of doubt
> “Questioning this would be irrational.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given how obvious this is, this position should be accepted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: confidence → certainty
Stylistic assurance is treated as epistemic guarantee.

### Transfer 2: assertiveness → truth
Tone substitutes for evidence.

### Transfer 3: rhetoric → authority
Persuasive language replaces justification.

### Transfer 4: lack of doubt → closure
Absence of hedging is mistaken for completeness.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If confidence replaces certainty, the system collapses:

- critical evaluation,
- openness to revision,
- distinction between persuasion and proof.

This produces **rhetoric-driven belief**, not justified knowledge.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Confidence is treated as **presentation style**, not evidence.
- Claims require **independent justification** regardless of tone.
- Doubt remains structurally admissible.
- Normative acceptance is argued beyond rhetorical strength.

If these conditions hold, confidence does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that confidence is bad,
- that assertive communication lacks value,
- that certainty is impossible,
- that hedging is always required.

It claims only:
- **confidence is not certainty**, and
- **rhetorical force does not prove truth**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- evidence-backed claims,
- probabilistic assertions,
- clearly scoped conclusions,
- arguments separating tone from justification.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing confidence loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- confidence becomes structurally equivalent to certainty, or
- doubt becomes inadmissible.
