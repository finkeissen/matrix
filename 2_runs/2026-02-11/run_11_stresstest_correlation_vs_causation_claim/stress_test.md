# Stress Test: Correlation-vs-Causation Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **correlation-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit causal or
normative authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_11_stresstest_correlation_vs_causation_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted correlation-claim injections)

### Injection A — causal leap
> “X and Y are strongly correlated, so X causes Y.”

### Injection B — explanatory substitution
> “This correlation explains the outcome.”

### Injection C — policy justification
> “Because X is associated with Y, X should be promoted.”

### Injection D — inevitability claim
> “Whenever X occurs, Y follows; therefore X is the reason.”

### Injection E — risk attribution
> “Y increases with X, so X is responsible.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: correlation → causation
A statistical relationship is treated as a causal link.

### Transfer 2: association → explanation
Observed co-variation substitutes for mechanism.

### Transfer 3: co-occurrence → reason
Temporal or statistical proximity is mistaken for cause.

### Transfer 4: statistic → norm
Descriptive association is converted into prescriptive action.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If correlation replaces causation, the system collapses:

- causal reasoning,
- intervention design,
- attribution of responsibility.

This produces **spurious explanation** and misguided action.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Correlations are treated as **descriptive patterns**.
- Causal claims require **mechanisms, interventions, or counterfactuals**.
- Alternative explanations (confounders, reverse causation) are considered.
- Normative actions are argued beyond statistical association.

If these conditions hold, correlation does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that correlations are uninformative,
- that statistics lack value,
- that causal inference is impossible,
- that policy should ignore data.

It claims only:
- **correlation is not causation**, and
- **association does not explain by itself**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit causal or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- purely descriptive correlations,
- causal hypotheses with proposed mechanisms,
- intervention-based evidence,
- policy arguments with explicit causal assumptions.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing statistical-causal loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- correlations are treated as self-justifying causes, or
- causal language becomes structurally implicit.
