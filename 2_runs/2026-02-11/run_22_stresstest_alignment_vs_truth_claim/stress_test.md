# Stress Test: Alignment-vs-Truth Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **alignment-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit epistemic
authority due to agreement or fit*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_22_stresstest_alignment_vs_truth_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted alignment-claim injections)

### Injection A — agreement as truth
> “This claim is correct because everyone involved agrees with it.”

### Injection B — value fit as evidence
> “This aligns with our values, therefore it is true.”

### Injection C — goal alignment
> “The result supports our objectives, so it must be correct.”

### Injection D — policy compliance
> “This follows the policy, hence it is valid.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given its alignment with our goals, this claim should be accepted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: alignment → truth
Agreement or fit substitutes for evidence.

### Transfer 2: value → validity
Normative congruence replaces factual justification.

### Transfer 3: goal → correctness
Instrumental usefulness is mistaken for truth.

### Transfer 4: compliance → authority
Rule-following is treated as epistemic validation.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If alignment replaces truth, the system collapses:

- independence of evaluation,
- capacity to detect inconvenient facts,
- distinction between goals and reality.

This produces **goal-driven validation**, not evidence-based judgment.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Alignment is treated as **normative or relational**, not evidentiary.
- Truth claims require **independent justification**.
- Goal fit and compliance do not substitute for evidence.
- Normative acceptance is argued beyond agreement.

If these conditions hold, alignment does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that alignment is irrelevant,
- that shared values lack importance,
- that policy compliance is unimportant,
- that goals should be ignored.

It claims only:
- **alignment is not truth**, and
- **agreement does not prove correctness**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit epistemic or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit alignment descriptions,
- independent truth claims with evidence,
- normative arguments separated from factual claims,
- policy compliance reports without epistemic elevation.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing alignment loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- alignment becomes a structural proxy for truth, or
- disagreement becomes epistemically inadmissible.
