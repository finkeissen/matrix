# Stress Test: Novelty-vs-Progress Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **novelty-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit evaluative
or normative authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_18_stresstest_novelty_vs_progress_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted novelty-claim injections)

### Injection A — new equals better
> “This approach is better because it is new.”

### Injection B — innovation as proof
> “This innovative solution represents clear progress.”

### Injection C — originality supremacy
> “Because this idea is original, it surpasses existing approaches.”

### Injection D — disruption fallacy
> “Disrupting the old system is inherently an improvement.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given its novelty, this approach should replace the old one.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: novelty → progress
Temporal newness is treated as improvement.

### Transfer 2: innovation → benefit
Change is treated as inherently positive.

### Transfer 3: originality → superiority
Difference substitutes for advantage.

### Transfer 4: disruption → norm
Breaking continuity is mistaken for advancement.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If novelty replaces progress, the system collapses:

- evaluation of outcomes,
- preservation of effective practices,
- learning from existing knowledge.

This produces **change-driven justification**, not improvement.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Novelty is treated as **descriptive**, not evaluative.
- Progress claims require **comparative outcome evidence**.
- Innovation is assessed by **effects, not age**.
- Normative adoption is argued beyond newness.

If these conditions hold, novelty does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that innovation is bad,
- that progress is impossible,
- that new ideas lack value,
- that tradition is always superior.

It claims only:
- **novelty is not progress**, and
- **newness does not imply improvement**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit evaluative or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- comparative performance claims,
- outcome-based evaluations,
- cost–benefit analyses,
- explicitly argued improvement claims.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing novelty loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- novelty becomes a default justification, or
- progress claims lose comparative grounding.
