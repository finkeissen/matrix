# Stress Test: Evidence-vs-Conclusion Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **evidence-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit conclusory
or epistemic authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_25_stresstest_evidence_vs_conclusion_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted evidence-claim injections)

### Injection A — data as conclusion
> “The data shows X, therefore X is true.”

### Injection B — indicator finality
> “This indicator proves the claim.”

### Injection C — observation collapse
> “We observed this effect, so the conclusion is settled.”

### Injection D — evidence as certainty
> “Given this evidence, there is no room for doubt.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Based on this evidence, this position should be adopted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: evidence → conclusion
Supporting material is treated as final claim.

### Transfer 2: data → truth
Raw observations substitute for inference.

### Transfer 3: indicator → result
Signals are mistaken for outcomes.

### Transfer 4: support → norm
Evidentiary presence is converted into prescription.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If evidence replaces conclusions, the system collapses:

- clarity of inference,
- transparency of reasoning,
- contestability of claims.

This produces **implicit reasoning**, not explicit justification.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Evidence is treated as **support**, not assertion.
- Conclusions require **explicit inferential steps**.
- Alternative interpretations remain admissible.
- Normative decisions are argued beyond evidentiary presence.

If these conditions hold, evidence does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that evidence is weak,
- that data lacks importance,
- that conclusions cannot be drawn,
- that inference should be avoided.

It claims only:
- **evidence is not a conclusion**, and
- **support does not equal proof**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit conclusory or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit inference chains,
- probabilistic conclusions,
- evidence-weighted arguments,
- normative proposals with stated reasoning.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing evidence loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- conclusions become implicit in evidence presentation, or
- inference steps become structurally invisible.
