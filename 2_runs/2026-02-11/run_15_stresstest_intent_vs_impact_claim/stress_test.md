# Stress Test: Intent-vs-Impact Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **intent-based statements** can be incorrectly admitted as
**claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit evidentiary
or moral authority*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_15_stresstest_intent_vs_impact_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted intent-claim injections)

### Injection A — good intention sufficiency
> “The policy was designed to help people, so it is beneficial.”

### Injection B — motive as justification
> “They meant well, therefore the outcome is acceptable.”

### Injection C — declared goal as fact
> “The program aims to reduce harm, so it reduces harm.”

### Injection D — sincerity as evidence
> “Because the intention was sincere, the impact must be positive.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the good intentions behind this decision, it should be supported.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: intent → impact
Stated motive is treated as evidence of outcome.

### Transfer 2: motive → effect
Purpose substitutes for measurable result.

### Transfer 3: sincerity → correctness
Moral framing replaces impact evaluation.

### Transfer 4: goal → achievement
Declared objective is mistaken for realized effect.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If intent replaces impact, the system collapses:

- outcome evaluation,
- accountability mechanisms,
- corrective feedback.

This produces **motive-driven justification** without evidence.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Intent and impact are explicitly distinguished.
- Outcomes are evaluated independently of motives.
- Moral evaluation does not substitute for empirical assessment.
- Normative support requires evidence of effect.

If these conditions hold, intention does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that intentions are irrelevant,
- that motives lack ethical importance,
- that good faith does not matter,
- that impact alone determines morality.

It claims only:
- **intent is not impact**, and
- **motive does not prove effect**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit evidentiary or moral authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit descriptions of intent,
- independent impact evaluations,
- causal analyses of policy outcomes,
- normative arguments distinguishing motive and effect.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing motive–impact loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- intention becomes a structural substitute for evidence, or
- outcome evaluation becomes secondary to motive framing.
