# Stress Test: Speed-vs-Quality Claim Injection

## Goal
Test whether **speed- or efficiency-based statements** can be incorrectly
admitted as **claims** (truth-apt artifacts), thereby acquiring *implicit
epistemic or evaluative authority due to rapidity*.

This document is the diagnostic output for:
- `2.runs/2026-02-11/run_20_stresstest_speed_vs_quality_claim`

Governing constraints:
- `Admissibility.md`
- `Stop_Rules.md`
- `ROLES.md`

---

## Test inputs (attempted speed-claim injections)

### Injection A — fast equals good
> “This result is better because it was produced quickly.”

### Injection B — efficiency as rigor
> “The process is efficient, so the outcome is reliable.”

### Injection C — latency as authority
> “Because the system responds instantly, it is more accurate.”

### Injection D — haste justification
> “There wasn’t time for review, but speed was more important.”

### Injection E — normative escalation
> “Given the fast turnaround, this solution should be adopted.”

---

## Diagnosis (isolatable illegitimate transfers)

### Transfer 1: speed → quality
Rapidity substitutes for correctness.

### Transfer 2: efficiency → rigor
Operational optimization replaces careful validation.

### Transfer 3: latency → accuracy
Response time is mistaken for precision.

### Transfer 4: haste → norm
Time pressure is treated as justification.

These transfers are structurally illegitimate and violate admissibility constraints.

---

## Why this matters
If speed replaces quality, the system collapses:

- error detection,
- robustness under review,
- long-term reliability.

This produces **haste-driven acceptance**, not careful evaluation.

---

## Counterfactual test (falsifiability)
No illegitimate transfer occurs if all of the following hold:

- Speed is treated as **operational**, not evaluative.
- Quality claims require **independent assessment**.
- Efficiency gains do not bypass validation.
- Normative adoption is argued beyond turnaround time.

If these conditions hold, speed does not distort admissibility.

---

## Scope limitations
This stress test does **not** claim:

- that speed is bad,
- that efficiency lacks value,
- that slow processes are superior,
- that rapid response is never desirable.

It claims only:
- **speed is not quality**, and
- **fast results do not prove correctness**.

---

## Decision / outcome

### Claim-admission decision
All injections (A–E) are **inadmissible as claims**.

Reason:
They introduce **implicit evaluative or normative authority**
that violates admissibility constraints.

### Acceptable reformulation
Some statements may become admissible if reformulated as:
- explicit performance timing reports,
- quality evaluations with evidence,
- trade-off analyses between speed and rigor,
- adoption arguments acknowledging review limits.

---

## STOP evaluation
No STOP was issued.

Rationale:
- All illegitimate transfers are isolatable.
- Counterfactual conditions are explicit.
- No self-sealing haste loop is introduced.

A STOP must be issued in future runs if:
- speed becomes a default proxy for quality, or
- review and validation are structurally bypassed.
